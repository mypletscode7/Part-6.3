Examination sources, tools and tasks

Sources

A variety of sources will feature in your assessment and will include:

  • a written extract from a literary or non-literary text
  • a multimedia text
  • static image(s).

Tools

Most questions will be answered using a standard text tool set. Other tools to facilitate different response types, such as flow charts, will be made available as required.

Task details

Analysing task

This task assesses your ability to analyse, then compare and contrast two unseen text extracts (one written text and one multimedia text). It is made up of a series of short response questions which focus on criterion A, followed by an extended response question which is assessed using criteria A and B. The task assesses your ability to:

  • analyse techniques used in written and multimedia texts, giving justifications for your opinions (criterion A)
  • organize your work in a coherent and logical manner (criterion B).

Producing literary text task

This is an extended creative writing task, with a stimulus of two static images provided. The task assesses your ability to:

  • produce a literary text that demonstrates imagination or sensitivity and an awareness of impact on your audience (criterion C)
  • produce text, demonstrating your ability to write in an appropriate register with grammatical accuracy (criterion D).

Producing non-literary text task

This is an extended non-literary writing task and is connected to the global context focus for the session and year of study. The task assesses your ability to:

  • organize your work in a coherent and logical manner (criterion B)
  • produce a non-literary text which demonstrates imagination or sensitivity and an awareness of impact on your audience (criterion C)
  • produce text, demonstrating your ability to write in an appropriate register with grammatical accuracy (criterion D).

On-screen examination

Task 1

This task assesses your comprehension of an audio-visual text in the target language. It is made up of a series of short response questions and assesses all strands of criterion A: Listening. All responses for all levels must be in the target language. A markscheme specific to the examination will be used to assess task 1.

Task 2

This task assesses your comprehension of a written-visual text in the target language. It is made up of a series of short response questions and assesses all strands of criterion B: Reading. All responses for all levels must be in the target language. A markscheme specific to the examination will be used to assess task 2.

Task 3

This task assesses your ability to produce written text to communicate messages and/or information in the target language. It is made up of two written prompts linked to the prescribed global context for the session. You choose one task to complete. The task is assessed using the external marking criteria below.

Internal assessment constitutes 25% of your language acquisition eAssessment. It takes the form of an individual speaking assessment and enables you to demonstrate the application of your linguistic skills and knowledge in an authentic setting. You are required to make an individual presentation in response to a stimulus, before engaging in one-to-one discussion with your teacher on the topic of the stimulus.

Task details

Investigating

While it is difficult to replicate an entire investigation within the constraints of the assessment, some of the discrete skills involved in completing the investigating task include:

  • formulating and justifying research questions
  • formulating action plans, or sections of an action plan
  • evaluating the process or results of an investigation.

You are also asked to demonstrate knowledge and understanding, either from your course or from information presented in source material.

Communicating

The communicating task requires you to engage creatively with a given topic or context, presenting information and ideas effectively using an appropriate style for your audience and purpose and in a way that is appropriate to the specified format.

Response types:

  • creative writing
  • blog
  • article
  • letter
  • presentation
  • poster/infographic
  • speech

Thinking critically

The final task assesses your ability to think about and discuss issues, arguments and perspectives through structured questions culminating in an extended piece of writing. You are also asked to demonstrate knowledge and understanding, either from your course or from information presented in source material.

Tools

A variety of response tools are available to you, including but not limited to an on-screen calculator, a measuring tool, drawing canvases, a graph plotter and a table drawing tool. You will be able to access an age-appropriate interactive version of the IUPAC periodic table used in Diploma Programme examinations. With the exceptions of copper and chlorine, the relative atomic masses of all elements will be rounded to the nearest whole number.

Tasks

Knowing and understanding

The first task assesses your knowledge and understanding of science; however, marks may be awarded against the other criteria when appropriate to the skills used in answering a question.

Investigation skills

The second task assesses the skills needed in scientific investigations (criteria B and C). The task may involve a single investigation or it may assess specific skills in a number of discrete scenarios. A variety of rich media will be used to present different challenges. You should expect to formulate hypotheses, plan investigations, collect data from simulations, present data appropriately and interpret and evaluate data and hypotheses.

Applying science

The third task requires you to explain how science is used to address a real-life issue. This task will be assessed against criterion D, but there may be occasions when marks are also awarded against the other three criteria when this is appropriate for the question.

Task details

The interdisciplinary learning on-screen examination (two hours) has two tasks; you should spend roughly one hour on each task. The structure of the examination ensures that you have time to provide comprehensive responses that demonstrate your creative and critical skills in an interdisciplinary setting. Reflecting will be assessed; however, the strand that will be assessed should not be limited to a specific task; it will depend on the context.

Evaluating and reflecting

The first task assesses your ability to analyse the interdisciplinary work and/or approach of someone else through source material. In your analysis, you will also evaluate the perspectives of the disciplinary and interdisciplinary approach in relation to a real-world issue present in the stimulus material.

Synthesizing and reflecting

The second task assesses your ability to synthesize disciplinary knowledge to demonstrate interdisciplinary understanding and your ability to use appropriate strategies to communicate interdisciplinary understanding effectively. You create your own, original, interdisciplinary piece(s) of work and explain the approach you have taken.

Questions could require:

  • reflect on learning experiences from relevant subject groups
  • justify your choice of disciplines
  • describe what understandings disciplines can bring to a real-world issue, idea or challenge, and how they challenge or complement each other
  • outline the nature of new understanding
  • apply new subject combinations to new contexts or topics/themes
  • apply existing subject combinations to related contexts or topics/themes
  • identify future actions you can take to extend your learning beyond the classroom
  • evaluate your own strengths and weaknesses as a learner.

Example: May 2025

An example of the Global Context being integrated into the on-screen examinations can be seen in the May 2025 examination series, which used the Global Context of Scientific and Technical Innovation.

In the English Language and Literature examination, specifically Task 3, candidates were presented with:

  • Global Context: Scientific and Technical Innovation
  • Assessment criteria: Criterion B (Organizing), Criterion C (Producing Text), and Criterion D (Using Language)
  • Task worth 30 marks (B 10, C 10, D 10)

Prompt:

Create an opinion piece for your local newspaper in which you propose an innovation that will improve the quality of life in your community.

The same Global Context was also used in Question 9 of Integrated Humanities:

Write a well-structured essay in response to the following question: To what extent can technological advances impact natural environments?

In your essay, you must:

  • reference at least one biome.
  • consider different perspectives.

Here, Scientific and Technical Innovation is again embedded directly into the assessment task. You will soon understand that one task is connected with, inspired by or derived from the selected global context.

But this is different for the interdisciplinary learning on-screen examination, which is completely inspired by the selected global context.

Interdisciplinary learning example

In the May 2025 Interdisciplinary Learning pre-release, the focus included smart technology, health-monitoring devices, personal data, privacy, and advertising.

These were connected to Scientific and Technical Innovation, exploring how technology is changing the way people monitor health, use digital platforms, and manage personal information.
